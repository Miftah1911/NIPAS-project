The Problem · Bangladesh

Bangladeshi smallholders face five compounding crises

These aren't abstract challenges — they are specific, measurable problems in Bangladesh today.

🚱

Groundwater depletion from flood irrigation

Northern districts (Rajshahi, Rangpur, Dinajpur) sit over aquifers that are being pumped dry. Water tables are dropping meters per decade. Tube wells now go 300+ feet deep.

💸

Over-fertilization & chemical waste

A smallholder spends 30–40% of total input cost on fertilizer and pesticide. Most is broadcast over the whole field — where 60–70% never reaches the plant. Runoff contaminates canals and kills soil biology.

🌱

Root zone damage from manual soil testing

Traditional digging with a spade cuts through the fine root hairs that absorb nutrients. Every cut is an infection entry point — a common cause of root rot in tomato and chili.

🍂

Diseases spread before they're visible

By the time a farmer sees chlorosis or necrosis, the disease has spread through the plant's vascular system. The window between invisible and unrecoverable is 3–5 days.

👴

Labor shortage & aging farmer population

Bangladesh's average farmer is over 50, and young people are migrating to cities for garment factory work. There aren't enough hands to walk every field every day.

The Solution · Problem by Problem

Every problem, a specific engineering fix

NIPAS was designed around these five problems — not around technology looking for a use case.

01
Groundwater depletion
Targeted micro-misting — delivers water only to each plant's root zone, only when soil moisture drops below threshold. Saves up to 70% of water.
02
Chemical overuse & waste
Spray only infected plants — ESP32-CAM scans every leaf, then doses only what's needed, at the exact amount required for that severity. 50% less chemical.
03
Root zone damage
Servo-deployed probe — capacitive sensor is lowered gently into soil by a metal-gear servo, then retracted. No digging, no cutting, no root damage.
04
Disease spreading undetected
HSV color analysis — the camera detects the color signature of infection at 5% ratio, well before the human eye can see yellowing or browning.
05
Labor shortage
Fully autonomous robot — scans every plant, every row, every 40 centimeters. 2–3 hours of work per charge, no human walking required.
06
No actionable farm data
Live web dashboard — every reading, every spray, every scan logged and streamed to the farmer's phone. Replaces guesswork with exact numbers.

Autonomy · The Brain

A state machine that drives the entire field mission

The robot cycles through 8 states autonomously. Every transition is published to the dashboard in real time.

⏸️
IDLE
Motors stopped. Waiting for START command.
➡️
MOVING
Driving forward. Watching encoder ticks + ultrasonic.
📷
SCANNING
Stop. Ask CAM for a sweep. Wait for HSV result.
💦
SPRAYING
Fire relay for duration proportional to infection severity.
🚧
OBSTACLE_AVOID
Back up, turn, retry. Escalate to ERROR if stuck.
↻
TURNING
Timed 180° spin at end of row. Self-corrects next pass.
✅
ROW_COMPLETE
Increment row counter. Move to TURNING or FINISHED.
🏁
FINISHED
All rows done. Slow LED blink. Awaiting reset.
Autonomy Factors

What makes the robot run on its own

📏

Odometry via Encoders

Distance tracked by wheel rotations. Every 20 ticks = one wheel revolution ≈ 20 cm.

  • Knows exact row position
  • Triggers scan at correct spacing
  • Detects row end without GPS
🚧

Reactive Obstacle Avoidance

Ultrasonic sensor continuously samples the path ahead. Any barrier within 15 cm triggers a state change.

  • Back up + turn sequence
  • Retries path after obstacle clears
  • Escalates to ERROR if stuck
🔄

Row-End Detection

Encoder ticks since row start compared against configured row length.

  • Auto turn on reaching end
  • Row counter increments
  • Field finish when all rows done
🎯

Adaptive Spray Dosing

Spray duration computed from infection severity — not fixed.

  • Healthy leaf → 0 ms spray
  • Mild infection → 800 ms
  • Severe infection → up to 2500 ms
🔗

UART Handshake with CAM

The CAM responds within a 3-second timeout. If it fails, robot assumes healthy and continues.

  • Never blocks indefinitely
  • Fails safe on CAM crash
  • Retries next plant
📡

MQTT Resilience

State published continuously. If WiFi drops, robot keeps working — messages queue for reconnect.

  • Offline-first design
  • QoS 1 guarantees delivery
  • Last Will announces disconnect

Honest Assessment

What we haven't solved yet

Being honest about limitations separates a real engineering project from a hobby demo.

📐

Approximate 180° turns

Without a gyro or compass, the turn is timed. Small drift accumulates over long rows — self-corrects but not perfect.

📷

HSV is sunlight-sensitive

Cloudy days shift the HSV values. A calibration step or AWB-locked camera would improve robustness.

🌾

No weed vs crop distinction

The camera can't tell a chili plant from a weed with similar hue. Would need shape analysis or a trained model.

🔋

Runtime limited by pump

The pump draws 1–2 A. Battery bank gives 2–3 hours of continuous field operation.

🧪

Single-tank chemical

Current design has one reservoir. Mixing fertilizer and fungicide would need a second pump + tank.

Future Scope · 1

Full custom PCB with everything on one board

The commercial mid-tier uses a custom terminal shield. The next step is a fully integrated single-board controller — a design target, not a shipping product today.

◈ Commercial Mid-Tier · Now
Terminal Shield
ESP32 sits on a custom terminal shield. External modules (motor driver, relay, servo) connect via screw terminals — no jumper wires.
  • Custom terminal shield PCB
  • External L298N motor driver
  • External relay + power modules
  • ≈৳7,180 total manufacturing cost
  • Field-ready but modular
  • Status: Design complete · not yet assembled
◈ Next Generation
Full Integrated PCB
A single custom PCB replaces the terminal shield, external motor driver, and separate power module. Fewer components, fewer failure points — same overall robot size.
  • Single ESP32-S3 with native camera interface
  • Onboard DRV8833 motor driver (replaces L298N)
  • Integrated 12V → 5V buck + TP4056 Li-ion charger
  • Machine-placed SMT replaces hand-soldered joints
  • Reduces wiring-related failures by an estimated 70–80%
  • ≈৳1,800 per PCB at 100-unit scale (parts + assembly only)
  • Status: Concept stage · 6-month roadmap

Note on cost: the ৳1,800 figure covers the PCB itself — not the chassis, motors, pump, tank, battery, or camera. A complete next-gen robot at 100-unit scale would still cost approximately ৳6,500–7,500. The real gain is reliability and manufacturability, not a dramatic price drop.

Future Scope · 2

Where NIPAS goes next

PHASE 01 · 3 MONTHS

Custom PCB Rev 1

Single-board controller with integrated motor driver, power management, and JST connectors. Eliminates 80% of wiring failures.

PHASE 02 · 6 MONTHS

MPU6050 Gyro + Precision Turns

Add a 6-DOF IMU for closed-loop heading control. 180° turns become exact, not timed. Row drift drops to < 1 cm.

PHASE 03 · 9 MONTHS

On-Device TinyML Disease Model

Replace HSV with a quantized MobileNet or YOLO-Nano model running on ESP32-S3. Detects weeds, distinguishes multiple diseases, works under any lighting.

PHASE 04 · 12 MONTHS

Sunlight-Adaptive Camera

Add ambient light sensor + servo-controlled diffuser. HSV ranges auto-calibrate based on measured illumination.

PHASE 05 · 18 MONTHS

Multi-Robot Swarm

Three or more NIPAS units share a single MQTT broker. Dashboard shows all robots on one field map. Collision avoidance via shared state.

PHASE 06 · 24 MONTHS

Solar + Swappable Battery

Roof-mounted 10W solar panel + hot-swap 18650 pack. Full-day runtime without grid charging. Truly off-grid.
